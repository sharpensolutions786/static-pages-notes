The number that surprises home loan buyers is total interest, not the EMI

An EMI is the fixed amount you pay every month until the loan is cleared. It bundles interest and principal, so the payment never changes even though the split inside it does. Early EMIs are mostly interest, later ones mostly principal.

Take a ₹50 lakh loan at 8.5% over 20 years. The monthly EMI is ₹43,391. The principal is ₹50,00,000, the total interest is ₹54,13,879, and the total payable is ₹1,04,13,879.

That is the figure that surprises most buyers. On a typical 20-year loan you often repay close to double what you borrowed, and the monthly EMI hides it.

Shorter tenure and a lower rate both cut the total sharply. Try your own loan amount, rate and tenure in the home loan EMI calculator, which shows the total cost alongside the EMI. The 8.5% here is an illustrative rate, not a quote.
