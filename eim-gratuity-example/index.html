How gratuity is worked out: a 12-year example

Gratuity is a lump sum paid when you leave a job after five years of continuous service. The Payment of Gratuity Act covers establishments with 10 or more employees. The five-year rule is waived only in case of death or disability.

The formula uses a 26-day month: last drawn basic plus DA, times 15 divided by 26, times years of service. With ₹60,000 basic plus DA and 12 years of service, that is 60,000 × 15/26 × 12 = ₹4,15,385.

Up to ₹20 lakh of gratuity is exempt from income tax over your lifetime, so that payout is tax-free. The limit is across all employers, not per job. Anything above it is taxed at your slab rate.

Two details help. A period over 6 months in the final year counts as a full year, so 12 years 7 months is treated as 13. And because the formula uses your last drawn basic, a promotion just before leaving raises the whole payout.
